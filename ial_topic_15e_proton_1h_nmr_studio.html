How to Read Any $^{1}\text{H}$ NMR Spectrum in 3 Steps

Master these three core clues to unlock full marks in IAL spectroscopy questions.

01

Clue 1: Signal Count

Number of Peaks = Number of $^{1}\text{H}$ Environments

Each distinct cluster/signal corresponds to a unique group of hydrogen atoms in different chemical environments. Symmetrical molecules exhibit fewer signals than total hydrogen count!

02

Clue 2: Peak Area / Integral

Integral Step Height $\propto$ Ratio of Protons ($n\text{H}$)

In $^{1}\text{H}$ NMR, the area under each peak or step height of the integration trace gives the relative number of hydrogen atoms in that specific environment (e.g., $3\text{H} : 2\text{H} : 1\text{H}$).

03

Clue 3: $(n+1)$ Splitting Rule

Multiplet Lines = Adjacent Protons + 1

High-resolution spectra split signals into fine sub-peaks caused by spin-spin coupling with $n$ hydrogen atoms on **neighbouring carbon atoms**.

🧪 Key Experimental Conditions (IAL Exam Must-Knows)

Tetramethylsilane (TMS) Standard Added as reference standard at $\delta = 0\text{ ppm}$. Contains 12 equivalent protons, producing a single sharp singlet well clear of organic shifts. Inert, volatile, non-toxic.
Proton-Free Solvents ($\text{CCl}_4$ / $\text{CDCl}_3$) Samples must be dissolved in solvents with no $^{1}\text{H}$ atoms so solvent peaks don't mask the sample. $\text{CCl}_4$ has no H; $\text{CDCl}_3$ uses deuterium ($^{2}\text{H}$ or $\text{D}$) which resonates outside $^{1}\text{H}$ range.
$\text{D}_2\text{O}$ Shake Test ($-\text{OH}$ / $-\text{NH}$) Lab technique to identify $-\text{OH}$ or $-\text{NH}$ protons. Shaking sample with heavy water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$) replaces labile $-\text{OH}$ with $-\text{OD}$, causing the peak to **disappear** from the spectrum!
