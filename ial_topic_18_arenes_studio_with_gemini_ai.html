Evidence for the Structure & Stability of Benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$)

Master the 3 core lines of evidence disproving Kekulé's cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene model in favor of the delocalised $\pi$-system.

Evidence 1 Thermochemical

Enthalpy Change of Hydrogenation ($\Delta H_{\text{hydro}}$)

Cyclohexene has 1 double bond: $\Delta H = -120\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.

Expected Cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene:
$3 \times (-120) = -360\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Actual Benzene Experimental:
$\Delta H = -208\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Benzene is $152\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ less exothermic (more stable) than expected. This extra stability is the delocalisation / resonance energy.

Mark Scheme Secret: State the exact values ($-360$ vs $-208$) and calculate the difference ($152\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
Evidence 2 X-Ray Diffraction

Bond Lengths & Planar Geometry

Kekulé's model predicts alternating single ($0.154\text{ nm}$) and double ($0.134\text{ nm}$) carbon-carbon bonds.

$\text{C-C Single Bond: } 0.154\text{ nm}$

$\text{C=C Double Bond: } 0.134\text{ nm}$

$\text{Benzene C--C Bonds: } 0.139\text{ nm (ALL EQUAL)}$

X-ray diffraction proves all 6 carbon-carbon bond lengths are equal and intermediate in length (bond order 1.5). Ring is a regular planar hexagon ($120^\circ$).

Mark Scheme Secret: Use the phrase "all carbon-carbon bond lengths are equal and intermediate between single and double bonds".
Evidence 3 Reactivity

Resistance to Electrophilic Addition

Alkenes with localized $\text{C=C}$ double bonds readily undergo electrophilic addition (e.g., decolourising bromine water).

$\text{Alkenes: Electrophilic Addition (Fast)}$

$\text{Benzene: Electrophilic Substitution Only}$

$\text{(Requires halogen carrier catalyst)}$

Benzene does NOT decolourise bromine water at room temp without catalyst. Addition would disrupt the stable $152\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ delocalised ring!

Mark Scheme Secret: "Substitution preserves the stable delocalised electron ring, whereas addition destroys it."

📊 Thermochemical Hydrogenation Energy Profile (Detailed Level Diagram)

Delocalisation Energy = 152 kJ/mol
📌 Mark Scheme Calculation Note:
$\text{Expected } \Delta H_{\text{hydro}} = 3 \times (-120\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}) = -360\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
$\text{Delocalisation Energy} = (-208) - (-360) = +152\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ (Benzene is $152\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ more stable than expected!).
Orbital Architecture

Bonding, Hybridisation & $\pi$-Delocalisation in Benzene

Understand the $sp^2$ hybridised framework, unhybridised $p$-orbital overlap, and formation of the continuous delocalised ring based on your lesson notes (`image_148733.jpg`).

Key Bonding Principles (Edexcel Mark Scheme Criteria):

1. Carbon Hybridisation ($sp^2$)

Each carbon atom undergoes $sp^2$ hybridisation, using 3 of its 4 valence electrons to form 3 single $\sigma$-bonds (2 with adjacent C atoms, 1 with a H atom). Bond angles are $120^\circ$ in a regular planar hexagon.

2. Unhybridised $p$-Orbitals

Each carbon atom retains 1 unhybridised $p$-orbital containing 1 electron, positioned perpendicular to the plane of the carbon ring.

3. Sideways $p$-Orbital Overlap

Rather than forming localized $\pi$-bonds between individual pairs, all 6 $p$-orbitals overlap sideways with both adjacent $p$-orbitals simultaneously.

4. Continuous Delocalised $\pi$-Cloud (6 Electrons)

The 6 $p$-electrons are delocalised throughout the ring, creating two doughnut-shaped rings of electron density above and below the plane of the carbon nuclei. This minimizes electron repulsion and lowers potential energy.

💡 Exam Note: Always specify that $p$-orbitals overlap sideways and that delocalisation occurs above and below the plane of the carbon ring.

Special Topic: Spec 18.6

Phenol Chemistry, Activation Mechanics & Acidic Properties

Master why Phenol reacts much faster with electrophiles than benzene, and contrast its acidity with aliphatic alcohols.

1. Why Phenol is More Reactive Than Benzene

No Catalyst Needed

• The lone pair of electrons on the Oxygen atom in the $-\text{OH}$ group delocalises into the benzene $\pi$-cloud.

• This dramatically increases the electron density of the aromatic ring, specifically at positions 2, 4, and 6 (ortho/para).

• The higher electron density polarises incoming electrophiles (like $\text{Br}_2$) without requiring a halogen carrier catalyst ($\text{FeBr}_3$).

Phenol Bromination with Bromine Water ($\text{Br}_2\text{(aq)}$):
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{Br}_2(\text{aq}) \xrightarrow{\text{room temp}} 2,4,6\text{-tribromophenol}(\text{s}) + 3\text{HBr}(\text{aq})$
Key Observations:

• Orange-brown bromine water decolourises (turns colourless).
• A white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol forms.
• Antiseptic / disinfectant smell given off.

2. Acidic Reactions & Sodium Phenoxide

Weak Acid

Phenol is a weak acid because the phenoxide ion ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$) is stabilized by delocalisation of the negative charge into the ring $\pi$-system.

a. Reaction with Active Metal (Sodium):
$2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{H}_2(\text{g})$

Observation: Sodium fizzes/dissolves, producing effervescence (gas bubbles of $\text{H}_2$) and forming a clear solution of sodium phenoxide.

b. Neutralization with Alkali ($\text{NaOH}$):
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Key Concept: Neutralization reaction! Phenol dissolves in $\text{NaOH}(\text{aq})$ to form a clear solution. Aliphatic alcohols (like ethanol) do NOT react with $\text{NaOH}$!

10-Reaction Master Summary with Edexcel Examiner Secrets

All 10 core IAL organic transformations enriched with mark scheme secrets, conditions, observations, and common candidate traps.

# Reaction Name Reagents & Conditions Balanced Chemical Equation Classification & Examiner Secrets / Traps 💡
1 Combustion of Benzene $\text{O}_2(\text{g})$, ignition $2\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + 15\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 12\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Combustion

Observation: Burns with a very smoky yellow flame due to high carbon-to-hydrogen ratio ($\text{C}:\text{H} = 1:1$) causing incomplete combustion and soot formation.
2 Halogenation (Bromination) $\text{Br}_2(\text{l})$, $\text{FeBr}_3$ (or $\text{Fe}$ / $\text{AlCl}_3$) catalyst, room temp $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Br} + \text{HBr}$ Electrophilic Substitution

Examiner Secret: Requires halogen carrier catalyst ($\text{FeBr}_3$) to polarize $\text{Br}_2$ and form $\text{Br}^+$. Observation: Steamy acidic fumes of $\text{HBr}$ released, liquid decolourises.
3 Nitration of Benzene Conc. $\text{HNO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, strictly $50^\circ\text{C}-60^\circ\text{C}$ $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ Electrophilic Substitution

Candidate Trap: Temperature must be kept at $50-60^\circ\text{C}$. Above $60^\circ\text{C}$, multiple nitration occurs (dinitration to 1,3-dinitrobenzene). Electrophile is $\text{NO}_2^+$.
4 Sulfonation of Benzene Fuming $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ ($\text{SO}_3$ in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$), warm $40^\circ\text{C}$ $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_3\text{H} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ Electrophilic Substitution

Examiner Secret: Electrophile is neutral sulfur trioxide ($\text{SO}_3$) or $^+\text{SO}_3\text{H}$. Produces benzenesulfonic acid.
5 Friedel-Crafts Alkylation Haloalkane ($\text{RCl}$), anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ catalyst, warm $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{RCl} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{R} + \text{HCl}$ Electrophilic Substitution

Candidate Trap: Must use anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ because water reacts violently with $\text{AlCl}_3$. Attached R group in final product carries NO positive charge!
6 Friedel-Crafts Acylation Acyl chloride ($\text{RCOCl}$), anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$, reflux $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{RCOCl} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COR} + \text{HCl}$ Electrophilic Substitution

Examiner Secret: Forms an aromatic ketone (e.g. phenylethanone). Bond forms directly between ring carbon and carbonyl carbon ($-\text{C=O}$). Electrophile: $\text{RCO}^+$.
7 Hydrogenation of Benzene $\text{H}_2(\text{g})$, $\text{Ni}$ catalyst, $150-200^\circ\text{C}$ $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}$ Electrophilic Addition

Examiner Secret: Converts benzene into cyclohexane. Destroys the $152\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ stable delocalised ring, hence requires high temp/pressure compared to alkenes.
8 Benzene + Halogen (UV) $\text{Cl}_2(\text{g})$, UV light (NO catalyst) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + 3\text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_6\text{Cl}_6$ Free-Radical Addition

Examiner Secret: Produces 1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane. No internal circle in product!
9a Phenol + Sodium Metal Sodium metal ($\text{Na}$), room temp $2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{H}_2$ Acid-Base / Redox

Observation: Sodium dissolves/fizzes with effervescence ($\text{H}_2$ gas), forming clear sodium phenoxide solution.
9b Phenol + Sodium Hydroxide $\text{NaOH}(\text{aq})$, room temp $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ Acid-Base Neutralization

Examiner Secret: Neutralization reaction! Phenol dissolves in $\text{NaOH}$ to form sodium phenoxide. Ethanol/aliphatic alcohols DO NOT react with $\text{NaOH}$!
10 Bromination of Phenol Bromine water $\text{Br}_2(\text{aq})$, room temp (NO catalyst) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{Br}_2 \rightarrow 2,4,6\text{-tribromophenol} + 3\text{HBr}$ Electrophilic Substitution

Observation: Orange bromine water decolourises AND a white precipitate forms. Oxygen lone pair delocalises into ring, activating positions 2, 4, 6.
