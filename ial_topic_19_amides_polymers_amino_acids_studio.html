Topic 19 (Part 2): Amides, Polymers, Peptides & Amino Acids Goals

Detailed breakdown of Edexcel IAL Specification points 19.7 through 19.11 with explicit mark scheme requirements.

19.7 Amide Preparation

Acyl Chlorides to Amides

  • Prepare primary amides using acyl chloride + concentrated ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$).
  • Prepare secondary amides ($N$-substituted) using acyl chloride + primary amine.
  • Prepare tertiary amides using acyl chloride + secondary amine.
  • Identify side product as ammonium or alkylammonium chloride salt ($[\text{RNH}_3]^+\text{Cl}^-$).
  • Recognize Paracetamol synthesis from 4-aminophenol and ethanoyl chloride.
19.8 Polymer Types

Condensation vs Addition

  • Describe condensation polymerisation releasing small molecules ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{HCl}$).
  • Form polyamides (e.g. Nylon 6,6, Kevlar, proteins) with amide linkages ($-\text{CONH}-$).
  • Form polyesters (e.g. Terylene/PET) with ester linkages ($-\text{COO}-$).
  • Describe addition polymerisation of propenamide to form poly(propenamide).
  • Outline 2-step route to poly(ethenol) via transesterification of poly(ethenyl ethanoate).
19.9 Repeat Units

Structural Formulae of Polymers

  • Draw structural formula of repeating units for Nylon 6,6 and Kevlar.
  • Draw repeat units for polyesters from dicarboxylic acids and diols.
  • Identify monomers from given polymer chain segments.
  • Draw single-monomer condensation polymers (from hydroxycarboxylic acids or amino acids).
  • Draw addition polymer repeat units including PVP and poly(propenamide).
19.10 Polymer Properties & Forces

Hydrogen Bonding & Liquitabs

  • Explain strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding in polyamides between $\text{C=O}$ and $\text{N-H}$.
  • Explain water solubility of poly(ethenol) due to extensive $-\text{OH}\cdots\text{H}_2\text{O}$ hydrogen bonding.
  • Describe commercial use in disposable laundry bags in hospitals to prevent contamination.
  • Describe commercial use in liquid-detergent capsules (liquitabs) that dissolve during washing.
  • Contrast acid/base biodegradability of condensation polymers vs non-biodegradable addition polymers.
19.11 Amino Acids, Peptides & TLC

pH Behaviour, Hydrolysis & TLC

  • Define zwitterions as dipolar ions with positive ($\text{H}_3\text{N}^+$) and negative ($\text{COO}^-$) charges.
  • Form peptide bonds ($-\text{CONH}-$) by condensation polymerisation to form dipeptides & tripeptides.
  • Hydrolyse peptides using reflux with $\text{HCl}(\text{aq})$ or $\text{NaOH}(\text{aq})$ to recover original amino acids.
  • Separate and identify amino acid mixtures using Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC) with ninhydrin locating agent.
  • Calculate retention factor $R_f = d_1 / d_2$ and match values against data tables.

Primary (1°) Amide

Ethanamide ($\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2$)

Formula: $\text{R-C(=O)-NH}_2$

Reagents: Acyl chloride + Conc. $\text{NH}_3$

Nitrogen atom is bonded to 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 carbonyl group.

Secondary (2°) Amide

$N$-Methylethanamide

Formula: $\text{R-C(=O)-NH-R'}$

Reagents: Acyl chloride + $1^\circ$ Amine

Nitrogen has 1 alkyl side-chain (prefixed with $N$-) and 1 hydrogen atom.

Tertiary (3°) Amide

$N,N$-Dimethylethanamide

Formula: $\text{R-C(=O)-N(R')}_2$

Reagents: Acyl chloride + $2^\circ$ Amine

Nitrogen is bonded to 2 alkyl side-chains (prefixed with $N,N$-) and NO hydrogen atoms.

Polymer Science Studio

Condensation vs Addition Polymers, Intermolecular Forces & Biodegradability

Explore polyamides, polyesters, hydrogen bonding strength, poly(ethenol) 2-step transesterification, and hospital laundry bag applications.

Condensation Polyamides

Nylon 6,6 & Kevlar (Amide Linkages)

Formed when dicarboxylic acids (or diacyl chlorides) react with diamines, eliminating small molecules ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{HCl}$) to create amide linkages ($-\text{CONH}-$).

Nylon 6,6 Repeat Unit: $-[\text{CO-(CH}_2\text{)}_4\text{-CO-NH-(CH}_2\text{)}_6\text{-NH}]_n-$

Kevlar Repeat Unit: $-[\text{CO-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-CO-NH-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-NH}]_n-$

💡 Intermolecular Forces & Physical Properties: Polyamides experience extensive hydrogen bonding between adjacent chains (from $\text{C=O}$ oxygen to $\text{N-H}$ hydrogen). This grants Kevlar its high tensile strength and high melting point for bulletproof armor!
Condensation Polyesters

Terylene / PET (Ester Linkages)

Formed when dicarboxylic acids react with diols, creating ester linkages ($-\text{COO}-$).

Terylene Repeat Unit: $-[\text{CO-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-CO-O-CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{-O}]_n-$

Monomers: Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + Ethane-1,2-diol

💡 Intermolecular Forces & Physical Properties: Polyesters contain polar carbonyl groups ($\text{C=O}$) forming permanent dipole-dipole forces and London dispersion forces between chains. Flexible, strong, and crease-resistant for clothing fabrics.
Special Addition Polymer

Poly(ethenol) 2-Step Synthesis & Hospital Liquitabs

Ethenol ($\text{CH}_2\text{=CH-OH}$) is unstable and tautomerises to ethanal. Poly(ethenol) must be made in 2 steps:

Step 1 (Addition Polymerisation):

Ethenyl ethanoate $\rightarrow$ Poly(ethenyl ethanoate)

Step 2 (Transesterification / Ester Exchange):

Poly(ethenyl ethanoate) $+\text{CH}_3\text{OH} \rightarrow$ Poly(ethenol) $+ \text{Methyl ethanoate}$

🌟 Commercial Applications: Poly(ethenol) contains abundant $-\text{OH}$ groups forming extensive hydrogen bonds with water molecules. Used in disposable hospital laundry bags (dissolves in hot wash to protect medical staff from contaminated bedding) and liquid-detergent capsules (liquitabs)!

🧩 Dipeptides & Tripeptides Combinatorics

When two different amino acids (A + B) react, two different dipeptides can form depending on which amino acid provides the $-\text{COOH}$ vs $-\text{NH}_2$ group:

Dipeptide 1 (Ala-Gly):
$H_2N-CH(CH_3)-CO-NH-CH_2-COOH$

Alanine $-\text{COOH}$ reacts with Glycine $-\text{NH}_2$.

Dipeptide 2 (Gly-Ala):
$H_2N-CH_2-CO-NH-CH(CH_3)-COOH$

Glycine $-\text{COOH}$ reacts with Alanine $-\text{NH}_2$.

⭐ Tripeptide Combinations: 3 different amino acids (e.g. Cysteine, Glycine, Threonine) yield 6 different tripeptides ($3! = 6$): Cys-Gly-Thr, Cys-Thr-Gly, Gly-Cys-Thr, Gly-Thr-Cys, Thr-Cys-Gly, Thr-Gly-Cys!

✂️ Peptide Linkage Hydrolysis (Acidic vs Alkaline Reflux)

Proteins and dipeptides can be hydrolyzed back into constituent amino acids by heating under reflux with concentrated acid or alkali:

1. Acidic Hydrolysis ($\text{HCl}(\text{aq})$, Reflux):
$-\text{CONH}- + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow -\text{COOH} + -\text{NH}_3^+$

Amide link breaks. Nitrogen group accepts $\text{H}^+$ forming ammonium cations ($-\text{NH}_3^+$) and carboxylic acid ($-\text{COOH}$).

2. Alkaline Hydrolysis ($\text{NaOH}(\text{aq})$, Reflux):
$-\text{CONH}- + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow -\text{COO}^- + -\text{NH}_2$

Amide link breaks. Carboxylic acid donates proton forming carboxylate anions ($-\text{COO}^-$) and free amine ($-\text{NH}_2$).

📋 Official IAL Mark Scheme TLC Protocol (3 Marks)

Step 1: Spotting

Dot small samples of amino acid mixture onto pencil baseline and dip bottom into solvent.

Step 2: Locating Agent

Amino acids are transparent/colorless. Spray with ninhydrin and heat for 10 min to produce purple/blue spots (or view under UV light).

Step 3: Distances

Measure distance from baseline to center of spot ($d_1$) and distance to solvent front ($d_2$).

Step 4: Identification

Calculate $R_f = d_1 / d_2$ and compare with known reference values in a data book.

Amides, Polymers, Peptides & Amino Acids Reaction Matrix

Complete IAL revision summary detailing equations, conditions, observations, and explicit IAL Examiner Secrets & Traps for all 16 reactions.

Reaction / System Reactants & Conditions Balanced Chemical Equation Key Observation / Properties ⚠️ IAL Examiner Secrets & Traps
1. Primary (1°) Amide Synthesis Acyl chloride $+$ Conc. $\text{NH}_3(\text{aq})$
Room Temp		 $\text{RCOCl} + 2\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{RCONH}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ Vigorous exothermic reaction, steamy white fumes of $\text{HCl}$ / solid $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$. ⚠️ 1:2 Mole Ratio Trap! 2 moles of $\text{NH}_3$ are required because released $\text{HCl}$ reacts immediately with a second mole of $\text{NH}_3$ to form $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$. Do not write 1:1 ratio!
2. Secondary (2°) Amide Synthesis Acyl chloride $+ 1^\circ$ Amine ($\text{R'NH}_2$)
Room Temp		 $\text{RCOCl} + 2\text{R'NH}_2 \rightarrow \text{RCONHR'} + [\text{R'NH}_3]^+\text{Cl}^-$ White solid secondary amide ($N$-substituted amide). ⚠️ Paracetamol Route: 4-aminophenol $+$ ethanoyl chloride $\rightarrow$ Paracetamol $+ \text{HCl}$. $-\text{NH}_2$ is a stronger nucleophile than $-\text{OH}$, so acylation occurs selectively at nitrogen!
3. Tertiary (3°) Amide Synthesis Acyl chloride $+ 2^\circ$ Amine ($\text{R}'_2\text{NH}$)
Room Temp		 $\text{RCOCl} + 2\text{R}'_2\text{NH} \rightarrow \text{RCONR}'_2 + [\text{R}'_2\text{NH}_2]^+\text{Cl}^-$ White solid/liquid $N,N$-disubstituted tertiary amide. ⚠️ 3° Amine Inactivity & 2:1 Ratio Trap: 1. Formed by reacting acyl chlorides with a secondary ($2^\circ$) amine. 2. Tertiary ($3^\circ$) amines CANNOT react to form amides because nitrogen lacks an $\text{H}$ to eliminate! 3. Requires a 2:1 mole ratio of amine to acyl chloride.
4. Polyamide: Nylon 6,6 Hexanedioic acid $+$ Hexane-1,6-diamine
Heat under Reflux		 $n\text{HOOC(CH}_2\text{)}_4\text{COOH} + n\text{H}_2\text{N(CH}_2\text{)}_6\text{NH}_2 \rightarrow -[\text{CO(CH}_2\text{)}_4\text{CONH(CH}_2\text{)}_6\text{NH}]_n- + 2n\text{H}_2\text{O}$ High melting point, strong fiber, excellent tensile strength. ⚠️ Eliminated Molecule Trap: Condensation of dicarboxylic acid $+$ diamine releases $2n\text{H}_2\text{O}$. If diacyl chloride is used instead, the side product is $2n\text{HCl}$!
5. Polyamide: Kevlar Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid $+$ Benzene-1,4-diamine $n\text{HOOC-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-COOH} + n\text{H}_2\text{N-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-NH}_2 \rightarrow -[\text{CO-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-CONH-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-NH}]_n- + 2n\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Bulletproof armor material, extremely high tensile strength & thermal stability. ⚠️ Structural Alignment Mark: Benzene rings are 1,4-disubstituted (para), ensuring linear chains that pack neatly for maximum intermolecular $\text{C=O}\cdots\text{H-N}$ hydrogen bonding.
6. Polyester: Terylene (PET) Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid $+$ Ethane-1,2-diol
Heat, Acid Catalyst		 $n\text{HOOC-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-COOH} + n\text{HO-CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{-OH} \rightarrow -[\text{CO-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{-COO-CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{-O}]_n- + 2n\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Flexible, crease-resistant clothing fibers & plastic bottles. ⚠️ Intermolecular Force Rationale: Polyesters experience permanent dipole-dipole forces between polar $\text{C=O}$ groups, NOT hydrogen bonding! Lower melting point than polyamides.
7. Single Monomer Polymerisation Hydroxycarboxylic acid or Amino acid monomer
Heat		 $n\text{HO-R-COOH} \rightarrow -[\text{O-R-CO}]_n- + n\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Biodegradable polyesters / polyamides for surgical sutures. ⚠️ Water Ratio Trap! Single monomer polymerisation eliminates only $n\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules (1 per repeat unit), whereas two-monomer systems eliminate $2n\text{H}_2\text{O}$!
8. Addition: Poly(propenamide) Propenamide monomer ($\text{CH}_2\text{=CHCONH}_2$)
Radical Initiator, Heat		 $n\text{CH}_2\text{=CH(CONH}_2\text{)} \rightarrow -[\text{CH}_2\text{-CH(CONH}_2\text{)}]_n-$ Water-absorbent hydrogel polymer. ⚠️ Commercial Application: Used in soft contact lenses and gel electrophoresis. Double bond opens (addition), while the amide side group remains intact!
9. Poly(ethenol) Step 1: Addition Ethenyl ethanoate ($\text{CH}_2\text{=CHOCOCH}_3$)
Radical Initiator		 $n\text{CH}_2\text{=CH(OCOCH}_3\text{)} \rightarrow -[\text{CH}_2\text{-CH(OCOCH}_3\text{)}]_n-$ Poly(ethenyl ethanoate) ester polymer. ⚠️ Why 2 Steps? Ethenol ($\text{CH}_2\text{=CH-OH}$) is unstable and spontaneously tautomerises to ethanal ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$). Direct addition of ethenol is impossible!
10. Poly(ethenol) Step 2: Transesterification Poly(ethenyl ethanoate) $+\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$
Heat, Catalyst		 $-[\text{CH}_2\text{-CH(OCOCH}_3\text{)}]_n- + n\text{CH}_3\text{OH} \rightarrow -[\text{CH}_2\text{-CH(OH)}]_n- + n\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_3$ Water-soluble polymer formed. ⚠️ Hospital Laundry Bags & Liquitabs: Extensive $-\text{OH}\cdots\text{H}_2\text{O}$ hydrogen bonding allows dissolution in hot wash, protecting hospital staff from dirty linen infection.
11. Addition: Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) $N$-vinylpyrrolidone monomer
Heat, Radical Initiator		 $n(\text{C}_6\text{H}_9\text{NO}) \rightarrow -[\text{C}_6\text{H}_9\text{NO}]_n-$ Water-soluble synthetic polymer. ⚠️ Monomer Identification Trap: Used in pharmaceutical binder pills & hairsprays. Identify $\text{C=C}$ bond on the vinyl group when deriving monomer from repeat unit!
12. Dipeptide Condensation Two $\alpha$-amino acids
Room Temp / Enzymatic		 $\text{H}_2\text{N-CH(R}_1\text{)-COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{N-CH(R}_2\text{)-COOH} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{N-CH(R}_1\text{)-CONH-CH(R}_2\text{)-COOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ Peptide linkage ($-\text{CONH}-$) formed. ⚠️ Combinatorics Trap! 2 different amino acids (A + B) form 2 different dipeptides (A-B and B-A). 3 different amino acids form 6 tripeptides ($3! = 6$).
13. Peptide Acidic Hydrolysis Dipeptide/Protein $+$ Dilute $\text{HCl}(\text{aq})$
Heat under Reflux		 $\text{R}_1\text{-CONH-R}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{R}_1\text{-COOH} + [\text{R}_2\text{-NH}_3]^+$ Amide bonds cleaved; protonated amino acid cations formed. ⚠️ Low pH Charge Trap: In acidic conditions ($\text{pH} < 2$), the amine group is fully protonated to $-\text{NH}_3^+$, while the carboxyl end stays $-\text{COOH}$. Net charge = $+1$.
14. Peptide Alkaline Hydrolysis Dipeptide/Protein $+$ Dilute $\text{NaOH}(\text{aq})$
Heat under Reflux		 $\text{R}_1\text{-CONH-R}_2 + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{R}_1\text{-COO}^- + \text{R}_2\text{-NH}_2$ Amide bonds cleaved; carboxylate anions formed. ⚠️ High pH Charge Trap: In alkaline conditions ($\text{pH} > 12$), carboxyl group donates proton forming $-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+$, while amine end stays $-\text{NH}_2$. Net charge = $-1$.
15. Amino Acid Zwitterion Shift Aqueous amino acid solution
Isoelectric Point (pI)		 $[\text{H}_3\text{N}^+\text{-CH(R)-COOH}] \xrightleftharpoons[\text{+H}^+]{\text{+OH}^-} [\text{H}_3\text{N}^+\text{-CH(R)-COO}^-] \xrightleftharpoons[\text{+H}^+]{\text{+OH}^-} [\text{H}_2\text{N-CH(R)-COO}^-]$ Dipolar zwitterion with net charge 0. High m.p. ($\approx 250^\circ\text{C}$). ⚠️ High Melting Point Rationale: Exceptionally high melting point is due to strong electrostatic ionic forces in a 3D zwitterion lattice, NOT simple hydrogen bonding!
16. TLC Amino Acid Locating Spray Ninhydrin on TLC plate
Heat for 10 minutes		 Ninhydrin $+$ Amino Acid $\xrightarrow{\Delta}$ Ruhemann's Purple complex Transparent spots turn purple/blue. ⚠️ 3-Mark TLC Protocol: 1. Amino acids are colorless $\rightarrow$ spray ninhydrin & heat 10 min to reveal spots. 2. Measure $d_1$ & $d_2$. 3. Calculate $R_f = d_1/d_2$ and compare with data book values.
