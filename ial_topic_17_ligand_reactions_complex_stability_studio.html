Spec 17.10 - 17.12 Coordination & Monodentates

1. Coordination Number & Monodentate Ligands

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Define **Coordination Number**: Total number of dative (coordinate) covalent bonds formed with central metal ion.
  • Classify Monodentate Ligands: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{OH}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$ (donate 1 lone pair).
  • Understand that neutral ligands ($\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{NH}_3$) do not change complex charge, whereas anionic ligands ($\text{Cl}^-, \text{OH}^-$) alter overall charge.
  • Explain colour changes resulting from changes in oxidation state, ligand type, or coordination number.
  • Deduce central metal oxidation state: $x + \sum (\text{ligand charges}) = \text{overall charge}$.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Coordination number is the number of DATIVE BONDS, NOT the number of ligand molecules! E.g., $[\text{Cr}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$ has 3 ligands, but its coordination number is 6 because ethanedioate is bidentate.

Spec 17.13 - 17.15 Complex Shapes & Cisplatin

2. 3D Complex Geometries & Cisplatin Isomerism

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Octahedral (CN=6): Formed with small ligands like $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $\text{NH}_3$ ($90^\circ$ bond angle).
  • Tetrahedral (CN=4): Formed with larger, bulky ligands like $\text{Cl}^-$ due to steric repulsion ($109.5^\circ$).
  • Square Planar (CN=4): Formed by $\text{Pt}^{2+}$ complexes ($90^\circ$ bond angle).
  • Linear (CN=2): Formed by $\text{Ag}^+$ complexes, e.g. $[\text{Ag}(\text{NH}_3)_2]^+$, $[\text{Ag}(\text{CN})_2]^-$.
  • Cisplatin $[\text{Pt}(\text{NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$: Anticancer drug supplied as a single $cis$-isomer to bind DNA; $trans$-isomer is ineffective and toxic.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Replacing $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with $\text{Cl}^-$ causes a change in coordination number from 6 to 4 ($[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} \rightarrow [\text{CuCl}_4]^{2-}$). State that $\text{Cl}^-$ is a larger ligand so fewer fit around the central metal ion!

Spec 17.16 - 17.17 Chelates & Haemoglobin

3. Bidentate/Hexadentate Chelates & Haemoglobin

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Bidentate Ligands: Form 2 dative bonds per ligand molecule (1,2-diaminoethane $\text{en}$, ethanedioate $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$).
  • Hexadentate Ligand: $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ forms 6 dative bonds per ligand molecule ($2 \times \text{:N}$, $4 \times \text{:O}^-$).
  • Chelate Effect: Monodentate replacement by multidentates increases entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$), producing more stable complexes.
  • Haemoglobin Structure: Contains an $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion in a multidentate porphyrin ring bound to globin protein, leaving 1 site for reversible $\text{O}_2$ binding.
  • Carbon Monoxide Toxicity: $\text{CO}$ forms a much stronger dative covalent bond with $\text{Fe}^{2+}$, replacing $\text{O}_2$ irreversibly.
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Always explain stability in terms of total entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}}$). Number of product moles > reactant moles $\rightarrow \Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$. Bond enthalpies are similar, so $\Delta H \approx 0 \rightarrow \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} \approx 0$.

Critical IAL Distinction Reaction Classification

4. Deprotonation vs Ligand Exchange (The #1 Student Misconception)

Stage 1: Deprotonation (Base Action)

Adding a small amount of aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{NH}_3$ to aqua-complexes $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$ causes deprotonation:

$[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4(\text{OH})_2](\text{s}) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$

⚠️ Misconception Alert: $\text{OH}^-$ ions DO NOT replace water ligands! They act as a base, removing $\text{H}^+$ protons from two $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ligands to form a neutral pale blue precipitate $\text{Cu}(\text{OH})_2(\text{s})$.

Stage 2: Ligand Exchange (Substitution)

Adding excess $\text{NH}_3$ to the blue precipitate causes ligand exchange:

$[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4(\text{OH})_2] + 4\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4(\text{H}_2\text{O})_2]^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$

✓ Observations: Blue precipitate dissolves to form a deep blue solution. $4\text{NH}_3$ molecules replace $2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and $2\text{OH}^-$ ligands.

Official IAL Thermodynamic Explanation:
  • Entropy of System ($\Delta S_{\text{system}}$): Positive ($\Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$) because number of moles increases ($\Delta n > 0$), creating significantly more disorder.
  • Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H$): Close to zero ($\Delta H \approx 0$) because the number and strength of dative bonds broken and formed are virtually identical.
  • Total Entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{total}}$): Strongly positive ($\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \frac{-\Delta H}{T} > 0$), making the chelate complex exceptionally stable!

Practical Applications of EDTA & Chelate Complexes

1. Heavy Metal Poisoning

$\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ binds poisonous heavy metal ions (e.g., $\text{Pb}^{2+}$, $\text{Hg}^{2+}$) in blood to form stable, non-toxic soluble chelate complexes excreted in urine.

2. Hard Water Softening

Added to shampoos and detergents to sequester $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions, preventing scum formation and allowing lathering.

📝 Chelate Effect Exam Writing Template:

"The reaction has an increase in entropy ($\Delta S_{\text{system}} > 0$) because the number of moles increases from 4 to 7. The enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) is close to zero because the number and types of dative bonds are similar. Therefore, $\Delta S_{\text{total}}$ is positive, making the complex formed very stable."

Haem Group ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$ Coordination Architecture)

Ligand Exchange Mechanics in Haemoglobin

1. Normal Oxygen Transport (Oxyhaemoglobin)

Oxygen forms a weak, reversible dative bond with $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ in the lungs where $\text{O}_2$ concentration is high. It travels through blood and releases $\text{O}_2$ readily to respiring tissues.

$\text{haemoglobin} + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{oxyhaemoglobin}$
📝 Official IAL Mark Scheme Explanation for CO Poisoning:

"Carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$) forms a much stronger dative covalent bond with the central $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion than oxygen ($\text{O}_2$). In a ligand exchange reaction, $\text{CO}$ replaces $\text{O}_2$ to form carboxyhaemoglobin. Because this bond is extremely strong and non-reversible, $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ can no longer transport oxygen to cells, leading to asphyxiation."
