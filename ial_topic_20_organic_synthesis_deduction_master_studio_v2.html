Analytical Spectroscopy Integration

  • Combustion Analysis (% C, H, N, O) $\rightarrow$ Empirical Formula $\rightarrow$ Molecular Formula via Mass Spec $M^+$.

  • ${}^1\text{H}$ & ${}^{13}\text{C}$ NMR: Environment count, chemical shift ($\delta$), splitting ($n+1$ rule), integration ratios.

20.3 Route Design & Risk

4-Step Planning & Practical Methods

  • Design up to 4-step pathways combining 3 years of organic chemistry.
  • Yield Multiplicative Effect: $\text{Yield}_{\text{total}} = y_1 \times y_2 \times y_3$. Fewer steps = Higher yield!
  • Racemic Mixtures: Addition to planar carbonyl groups ($\text{C=O}$) gives equal 50:50 probability of attack.
  • Purification: Reflux, distillation, recrystallisation, melting point purity determination.

Carbon Chain Engineering

"Special Techniques" for Changing Carbon Chain Lengths

Detailed mechanism explanations and IAL mark scheme secrets for $+1\text{C}$, $+\text{many C}$, and $-1\text{C}$ transformations.

a. Haloalkane Nucleophilic Substitution ($+1\text{C}$)
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{KCN} \xrightarrow{\text{Ethanol, Reflux}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CN} + \text{KCl}$

Mechanism: $S_N2$ Nucleophilic Substitution by cyanide ion ($:\text{C}\equiv\text{N}^-$).

⚠️ Examiner Trap: Solvent must be ethanol! Water causes hydroxide competition to form alcohols.

b. Hydroxynitrile Formation ($+1\text{C}$)
$\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{HCN} \xrightarrow{\text{KCN, pH } 8} \text{CH}_3\text{CH(OH)CN}$

Mechanism: Nucleophilic addition to planar carbonyl group.

💡 Racemic Mixture Trap: Equal 50:50 attack probability from top or bottom forms optically inactive racemic mixture.

c. Friedel-Crafts Benzene Alkylation ($+\text{Many C}$)
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{R-Cl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous AlCl}_3, \text{Warm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-R} + \text{HCl}$
d. Friedel-Crafts Benzene Acylation ($+\text{Many C}$)
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{R-COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{Anhydrous AlCl}_3, \text{Warm}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-COR} + \text{HCl}$
e. Tri-iodomethane / Iodoform Reaction ($-1\text{C}$ Chain Shortening)
$\text{R-COCH}_3 + 3\text{I}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R-COO}^-\text{Na}^+ + \text{CHI}_3\downarrow + 3\text{NaI} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Observation: Pale yellow precipitate of tri-iodomethane ($\text{CHI}_3$). Cleaves off the methyl carbon!

Hydrocarbons & Aliphatic Conversions (Complete 13 Equations)

13/13 Equations Mapped
1. Alkane $\rightarrow$ Monohaloalkane
$\text{CH}_4 + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{UV Light / Sunlight}} \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}$

Conditions: $\text{Cl}_2/\text{Br}_2$, UV Light, r.t. Mechanism: Free Radical Substitution (Initiation, Propagation, Termination). Observation (Cl$_2$): Green-yellow $\text{Cl}_2$ is consumed; steamy acidic fumes of $\text{HCl}$ in moist air. ⚠️ Examiner Trap: Excess alkane is required to avoid multi-substituted side products ($\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2, \text{CHCl}_3$).

Learning goals
  • State reagents ($\text{Cl}_2/\text{Br}_2$) and UV for free radical substitution of alkanes.
  • Outline initiation, propagation and termination stages.
  • Explain why excess alkane limits polysubstitution.
Examiner tips
  • Missing UV / sunlight loses marks.
  • Polysubstitution products ($\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{CHCl}_3$) if halogen is in excess.
  • Do not write electrophilic substitution for alkanes.

Safety: Halogens and UV: handle in fume cupboard; avoid breathing $\text{Cl}_2/\text{Br}_2$.

2. Alkene $\rightarrow$ Alkane
$\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni}, 150^\circ\text{C}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_3$

Conditions: $\text{H}_2(\text{g})$, Nickel catalyst at $150^\circ\text{C}$ (or Platinum/Palladium at r.t.). Mechanism: Heterogeneous Catalytic Addition.

Learning goals
  • State catalytic hydrogenation: $\text{H}_2$ with Ni (heat) or Pt/Pd (r.t.).
  • Recognise alkene → alkane as addition / reduction of the $\text{C=C}$.
Examiner tips
  • Must name a catalyst — $\text{H}_2$ alone is incomplete.
  • Ni typically needs ~$150^\circ\text{C}$; Pt/Pd can work at room temperature.
3. Alkene $\rightarrow$ Monohaloalkane
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH=CH}_2 + \text{HBr}(\text{g}) \xrightarrow{\text{r.t.}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH(Br)CH}_3 \text{ (Major product)}$

Mechanism: Electrophilic Addition. ⚠️ Markovnikov Rule: $\text{H}^+$ adds to carbon with more hydrogen atoms, forming the major product via the more stable $2^\circ/3^\circ$ carbocation intermediate.

Learning goals
  • State electrophilic addition of $\text{HX}$ to alkenes.
  • Apply Markovnikov’s rule for unsymmetrical alkenes (major product).
Examiner tips
  • Major product via the more stable carbocation ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$).
  • Do not confuse with free radical addition of $\text{HBr}$/peroxide (anti-Markovnikov — often beyond core).
4. Haloalkane $\rightarrow$ Alkene
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + \text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\text{Ethanolic, Reflux}} \text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + \text{KBr} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Mechanism: Elimination ($E_2$). ⚠️ Trap: Must use ethanolic KOH! Aqueous KOH gives alcohol substitution instead.

Learning goals
  • State ethanolic $\text{KOH}$, heat under reflux for elimination to alkene.
  • Contrast elimination (ethanolic) with substitution (aqueous).
Examiner tips
  • Classic trap: aqueous $\text{KOH}$ → alcohol, not alkene.
  • Must specify ethanolic solvent for elimination.

Safety: Hot alkali: corrosive — handle carefully.

5. Alkene $\rightarrow$ Alkanol (Hydration)
$\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g}) \xrightarrow{\text{Conc. H}_3\text{PO}_4, 300^\circ\text{C}, 60\text{ atm}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$

Conditions: Steam $\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g})$, conc. $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ catalyst on silica, $300^\circ\text{C}, 60\text{ atm}$. Electrophilic Addition.

Learning goals
  • State steam hydration conditions: conc. $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$, high $T$ and $P$.
  • Identify product as an alkanol (alcohol) from alkene.
Examiner tips
  • Do not confuse industrial hydration with cold dilute alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ (diol).
  • Alternative lab path: $\text{HX}$ then aqueous $\text{KOH}$ (two steps).
6. Alkanol $\rightarrow$ Alkene (Dehydration)
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Conc. H}_3\text{PO}_4, 170^\circ\text{C}} \text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Conditions: Concentrated $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ or conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, heat at $170^\circ\text{C}$. Acid-catalysed Elimination.

Learning goals
  • State acid-catalysed dehydration: conc. $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ or $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, ~$170^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Recognise elimination reverse of hydration.
Examiner tips
  • Temperature matters — lower $T$ can favour substitution products in some contexts.
  • State concentrated acid, not dilute.

Safety: Concentrated acids are corrosive.

7. Alkene $\rightarrow$ Diol
$\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + [\text{O}] + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Cold dilute alkaline KMnO}_4} \text{CH}_2(\text{OH})\text{CH}_2(\text{OH})$

Observation: Cold dilute alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ (school): purple decolourises; product is a diol. (Hot/acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ can cleave the C=C.) Unsaturation test!

Learning goals
  • State cold dilute alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ for diol formation.
  • Link purple decolourisation to unsaturation test / oxidation.
Examiner tips
  • Hot / acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ can cleave the $\text{C=C}$ — not the school diol route.
  • Product is a diol (glycol), not a monohydric alcohol.

Safety: $\text{KMnO}_4$ stains and is oxidising — avoid skin contact.

8. Alkene $\rightarrow$ Dihaloalkane
$\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{r.t.}} \text{CH}_2\text{BrCH}_2\text{Br}$

Test: Orange-brown bromine water decolourises (unsaturation). Synthesis: pure 1,2-dibromoalkane usually uses $\text{Br}_2$ in organic solvent; with water, bromohydrin can form.

Learning goals
  • State $\text{Br}_2$ addition to give 1,2-dihaloalkane.
  • Use bromine decolourisation as a test for $\text{C=C}$.
Examiner tips
  • For pure 1,2-dibromoalkane, $\text{Br}_2$ in organic solvent is preferred; water can give bromohydrin.
  • Observation: orange-brown bromine decolourises.

Safety: Bromine is toxic/corrosive — fume cupboard.

9. Dihaloalkane $\rightarrow$ Alkyne
$\text{CH}_2\text{BrCH}_2\text{Br} + 2\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\text{Ethanolic KOH, Reflux}} \text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + 2\text{KBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Type: Double Elimination removing 2 moles of $\text{HX}$ per mole of dihaloalkane.

Learning goals
  • State double elimination: vicinal dihaloalkane + ethanolic $\text{KOH}$, reflux → alkyne.
  • Recall 2 mol $\text{HX}$ removed per mole of dihaloalkane.
Examiner tips
  • Ethanolic (not aqueous) base is required for elimination.
  • Starting dihalide is usually from alkene + $\text{Br}_2$ (Rxn 8).
10. Haloalkane $\rightarrow$ Alkanol
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + \text{KOH}(\text{aq}) \xrightarrow{\text{Reflux}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{KBr}$

Mechanism: Nucleophilic Substitution ($S_N2$ for $1^\circ$, $S_N1$ for $3^\circ$). Rate: $\text{C-I} > \text{C-Br} > \text{C-Cl}$ due to bond enthalpy.

Learning goals
  • State aqueous $\text{KOH}$, reflux for nucleophilic substitution to alkanol.
  • Link rate order $\text{C–I} > \text{C–Br} > \text{C–Cl}$ to bond enthalpy.
Examiner tips
  • Trap: ethanolic $\text{KOH}$ → elimination (alkene), not alcohol.
  • $1^\circ$ often $S_N2$; $3^\circ$ often $S_N1$ — mention if asked.
11. Alkanol $\rightarrow$ Chloroalkane ($\text{PCl}_5$)
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{PCl}_5 \xrightarrow{\text{r.t. (Dry)}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + \text{POCl}_3 + \text{HCl}(\text{g})$

Observation: Dense steamy acidic white fumes of $\text{HCl}(\text{g})$ gas. Functional group test for $-\text{OH}$ group!

Learning goals
  • State $\text{PCl}_5$ (dry) converts alcohol → chloroalkane + $\text{POCl}_3$ + $\text{HCl}(\text{g})$.
  • Use steamy $\text{HCl}$ fumes as a test for $-\text{OH}$.
Examiner tips
  • Must be dry — water hydrolyses $\text{PCl}_5$.
  • Observation of dense white/steamy acidic fumes is a mark point.

Safety: $\text{PCl}_5$ reacts violently with water; corrosive fumes.

12. Alkanol $\rightarrow$ Bromoalkane ($\text{KBr/H}_2\text{SO}_4$)
$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{HBr} \xrightarrow{\text{KBr} + 50\% \text{ H}_2\text{SO}_4, \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

In-situ Reaction: $\text{KBr} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{KHSO}_4 + \text{HBr}$. Avoid 100% conc $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ as it oxidizes $\text{HBr}$ to $\text{Br}_2$.

Learning goals
  • State in-situ $\text{HBr}$ from $\text{KBr}$ + (approx. 50%) $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ for bromoalkane.
  • Write alcohol + $\text{HBr}$ → bromoalkane + water.
Examiner tips
  • Avoid 100% conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: oxidises $\text{HBr}$ to $\text{Br}_2$.
  • Name both $\text{KBr}$ and acid when asked for reagents.

Safety: Acid mixture is corrosive.

13. Alkanol $\rightarrow$ Iodoalkane ($\text{Red P/I}_2$)
$3\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{PI}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{Red P } + \text{I}_2, \Delta} 3\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{I} + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_3$

In-situ Reaction: $2\text{P} + 3\text{I}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{PI}_3$. $\text{PI}_3$ is prepared in-situ because it is unstable.

Learning goals
  • State red phosphorus + $\text{I}_2$ generate $\text{PI}_3$ in situ for iodoalkane.
  • Write balanced idea: $3\text{ROH} + \text{PI}_3$ → $3\text{RI} + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_3$.
Examiner tips
  • $\text{PI}_3$ is prepared in situ because it is unstable.
  • Do not invent aqueous $\text{HI}$ as the only school route if red P/$\text{I}_2$ is expected.

⚡ Cycle 1 High-Yield Examiner Trap Pair Masterclass
Trap Pair 1: Ethanolic KOH vs Aqueous KOH

Ethanolic KOH: Acts as a strong base $\rightarrow$ Elimination ($E_2$) forming Alkenes.
Aqueous KOH: Hydrated $\text{OH}^-$ acts as a nucleophile $\rightarrow$ Nucleophilic Substitution forming Alkanols.

Trap Pair 2: Reversible Hydration vs Acid Dehydration

Hydration: Steam $\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g}) + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4, 300^\circ\text{C}, 60\text{atm} \rightarrow \text{Alkanol}$.
Dehydration: Excess Conc $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4, 170^\circ\text{C} \rightarrow \text{Alkene} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.

1. Primary (1°) Alcohol Oxidation $\rightarrow$ Aldehyde
$\text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + [\text{O}] \xrightarrow{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}/\text{H}^+, \text{Distill Immediately}} \text{RCHO} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Reactants / products: $1^\circ$ alcohol $\rightarrow$ aldehyde (+ water).

Conditions: Acidified potassium dichromate; distil the aldehyde as it forms so it is not oxidised further.

Observation: Orange $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ turns green ($\text{Cr}^{3+}$).

Type: Oxidation (partial) of primary alcohol.

Learning goals
  • State reagents and why distillation is used for aldehyde preparation.
  • Link colour change of dichromate to oxidation occurring.
  • Distinguish this path from reflux → carboxylic acid.
Examiner tips
  • If you reflux with excess oxidant you get the acid, not the aldehyde.
  • Must say acidified dichromate (or acidified chromate), not just “oxidising agent”.
  • Distil immediately is a classic mark point.

Safety: Acidified dichromate is toxic/corrosive — handle with care; use fume cupboard if concentrated acid fumes.

2. Aldehyde Reduction $\rightarrow$ Primary (1°) Alcohol
$\text{RCHO} + 2[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{NaBH}_4(\text{aq})} \text{RCH}_2\text{OH}$

Reactants / products: Aldehyde $\rightarrow$ $1^\circ$ alcohol ($2[\text{H}]$).

Reagents: Aqueous $\text{NaBH}_4$ (hydride reducing agent).

Type: Nucleophilic addition of hydride $:\text{H}^-$, then protonation.

Learning goals
  • Recall $\text{NaBH}_4$ reduces aldehydes (and ketones) but not carboxylic acids.
  • Write reduction as $+2[\text{H}]$ for aldehyde $ ightarrow$ alcohol.
Examiner tips
  • Do not write $\text{LiAlH}_4$ as the only answer if $\text{NaBH}_4$ is acceptable and safer in water.
  • Product of aldehyde reduction is always a primary alcohol.
3. Aldehyde Oxidation $\rightarrow$ Alkanoic Acid
$\text{RCHO} + [\text{O}] \xrightarrow{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}/\text{H}^+, \text{Heat under Reflux}} \text{RCOOH}$

Reactants / products: Aldehyde $\rightarrow$ alkanoic acid.

Conditions: Excess acidified dichromate, heat under reflux.

Observation: Orange → green.

Learning goals
  • State reflux + excess oxidant for complete oxidation to acid.
  • Know aldehydes are oxidised further; ketones are not (under these conditions).
Examiner tips
  • Tollens’/Fehling’s also oxidise aldehydes (silver mirror / brick-red) — useful identification.
  • Do not confuse with controlled distillation used to make aldehydes from $1^\circ$ alcohols.

Safety: Hot acidified dichromate: corrosive; avoid skin contact.

4. Alkanoic Acid Reduction $\rightarrow$ Primary (1°) Alcohol
$\text{RCOOH} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4, \text{Dry Ether, then } \text{H}^+(\text{aq})} \text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Reactants / products: Acid $\rightarrow$ $1^\circ$ alcohol ($4[\text{H}]$).

Reagents: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in dry ether, then dilute acid work-up.

Trap: $\text{NaBH}_4$ is too weak to reduce $\text{RCOOH}$.

Learning goals
  • State $\text{LiAlH}_4$ + dry ether + acid work-up for acid → $1^\circ$ alcohol.
  • Contrast with $\text{NaBH}_4$ (carbonyls only).
Examiner tips
  • Missing “dry ether” or acid work-up loses marks.
  • Do not claim $\text{NaBH}_4$ reduces carboxylic acids.

Safety: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ reacts violently with water — keep dry; quench carefully under supervision.

5. Secondary (2°) Alcohol Oxidation $\rightarrow$ Ketone
$\text{R-CH(OH)-R'} + [\text{O}] \xrightarrow{\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}/\text{H}^+, \text{Reflux}} \text{R-CO-R'} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Reactants / products: $2^\circ$ alcohol $\rightarrow$ ketone (+ water).

Conditions: Acidified dichromate, heat under reflux.

Observation: Orange → green.

Trap: Product is ketone — does not oxidise further to acid under IAL dichromate conditions.

Learning goals
  • Link $2^\circ$ alcohol oxidation specifically to ketone.
  • Explain why further oxidation to acid does not occur (no aldehydic H).
Examiner tips
  • Do not write “carboxylic acid” as the product of $2^\circ$ alcohol oxidation.
  • State reflux/conditions clearly.

Safety: Acidified dichromate: toxic/corrosive.

6. Ketone Reduction $\rightarrow$ Secondary (2°) Alcohol
$\text{R-CO-R'} + 2[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{NaBH}_4(\text{aq})} \text{R-CH(OH)-R'}$

Reactants / products: Ketone $\rightarrow$ $2^\circ$ alcohol ($2[\text{H}]$).

Reagents: Aqueous $\text{NaBH}_4$.

Type: Nucleophilic addition of hydride.

Learning goals
  • State ketone $+ \text{NaBH}_4$ → $2^\circ$ alcohol.
  • Distinguish from aldehyde reduction (→ $1^\circ$ alcohol).
Examiner tips
  • Product is secondary, not primary.
  • Ketones are not oxidised by acidified dichromate under normal IAL conditions.

6. Ketone → 2° Alcohol

Product FG: $2^\circ$ alcohol

Reagent: $\text{NaBH}_4(\text{aq})$

[H] count idea: $+2[\text{H}]$

Compare: Aldehyde + NaBH₄ → 1° alcohol

⚡ Cycle 2 High-Yield Examiner Traps

NaBH₄ vs LiAlH₄: $\text{NaBH}_4(\text{aq})$ reduces aldehydes/ketones only. $\text{LiAlH}_4$ is needed for carboxylic acids and must be in dry ether, then dilute acid work-up.

Distil vs reflux (1° alcohol): Distil immediately → stop at aldehyde. Reflux with excess oxidant → push to acid.

Dichromate colour: Orange $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ → green $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ shows oxidation occurred.

3° Alcohol: No H on C–OH carbon → no oxidation with acidified dichromate under IAL conditions.

Ketones vs aldehydes: Ketones resist further oxidation by dichromate; aldehydes oxidise to acids (Tollens’/Fehling’s distinguish).

Also produces: $\text{POCl}_3$.

$\text{RCOCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{r.t.}} \text{RCOOH} + \text{HCl}(\text{g})$

Reagent: Water (vigorous at r.t.).

Products: Acid + HCl(g).

Observation: Steamy fumes of HCl; exothermic.

Learning goals
  • State hydrolysis of RCOCl with water.
  • Predict HCl as inorganic product.
Examiner tips

5. Nitrile $\rightarrow$ Alkanoic Acid (acid hydrolysis)

Examiner tips
  • Do not write NH₃ as product under acidic conditions — NH₄⁺ forms.

6. Acid + Alcohol $\rightarrow$ Ester (esterification)
$\text{RCOOH} + \text{R'OH} \xrightleftharpoons[\Delta]{\text{Conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{RCOOR'} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Products: Ester + water.

Learning goals
  • Write condensation of acid + alcohol → ester + water.
  • State conc. H₂SO₄ and equilibrium nature.
Examiner tips
  • Name ester from acid stem + alcohol alkyl (e.g. ethanoic + methanol → methyl ethanoate).
  • Not as complete as acyl chloride + alcohol.

Safety: Conc. H₂SO₄ highly corrosive.

7. Acyl Chloride + Alcohol $\rightarrow$ Ester
$\text{RCOCl} + \text{R'OH} \xrightarrow{\text{r.t.}} \text{RCOOR'} + \text{HCl}(\text{g})$

Conditions: Room temperature; no catalyst needed.

Products: Ester + HCl(g).

Advantage: Irreversible / goes to completion (vs esterification equilibrium).

Learning goals
  • State RCOCl + alcohol → ester + HCl.
  • Explain why this route is preferred for high yield.
Examiner tips
  • Faster and not equilibrium-limited compared with acid + alcohol.
  • HCl fumes observed.

Safety: HCl fumes; acyl chlorides moisture-sensitive.

8. Acyl Chloride + NH₃ $\rightarrow$ Primary (1°) Amide
$\text{RCOCl} + 2\text{NH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{r.t.}} \text{RCONH}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$

Why 2 NH₃: One forms the amide; one neutralises HCl.

Learning goals
  • State 2:1 mole ratio NH₃ : RCOCl.
  • Identify products 1° amide + ammonium chloride.
Examiner tips
  • Classic stoichiometry trap — using only 1 mol NH₃ is wrong.
  • Primary amide has –CONH₂.

Safety: Ammonia and HCl fumes — ventilation.

9. Acyl Chloride + Primary Amine $\rightarrow$ Secondary (2°) Amide

Learning goals
  • State 2 mol amine per 1 mol acyl chloride.
  • Identify 2° amide + amine salt products.
Examiner tips
  • Same 2:1 base rule as ammonia.
  • Do not call product a primary amide.

Safety: Amines and HCl fumes — good ventilation.

⚠️ Master Trap Checklist cross-cutting ▼
  • Ethanolic vs aqueous KOH: ethanolic → elimination (alkene); aqueous → substitution (alcohol).
  • Distil vs reflux (1° alcohol oxidation): distil immediately → aldehyde; reflux / excess oxidant → acid.
  • NaBH₄ vs LiAlH₄: NaBH₄ reduces aldehydes/ketones only; acids need LiAlH₄ (dry ether + acid work-up).
  • Markovnikov: HX addition — major product via more stable carbocation (X on C with fewer H).
  • Dry conditions: PCl₅, LiAlH₄, Grignard (Mg / dry ether) — water ruins the reaction.
  • KCN solvent: ethanolic for nitrile (+1 C); aqueous OH⁻ competes to give alcohol.
  • Amide / amine stoichiometry with RCOCl: typically 2 : 1 base : acyl chloride.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis products: carboxylate salt (+ alcohol / NH₃ / amine) — free acid only after acidification.
  • 2° alcohol oxidation: stops at ketone under IAL dichromate (not carboxylic acid).
  • 3° alcohols: not oxidised by acidified dichromate; made via Grignard + ketone.
  • Iodoform: needs CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)–; pale yellow CHI₃ ppt; chain shortens by 1 C.
  • Grignard + water: RMgX + H₂O → RH (destroys reagent) — apparatus must be dry.
