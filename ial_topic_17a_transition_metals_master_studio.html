Spec 17.1 - 17.3 Definitions & Configurations

1. Transition Metal Definition & Variable Oxidation States

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Define a Transition Metal: "A $d$-block element that forms one or more stable ions with an incompletely filled $d$-orbital."
  • Deduce electronic configurations for Period 4 elements ($\text{Sc}$ to $\text{Zn}$) and their ions.
  • Remember the $4s$ removal rule: When forming ions, lose $4s$ electrons BEFORE $3d$ electrons!
  • Explain variable oxidation states: $3d$ and $4s$ energy levels are very close in energy.
  • Prove why $\text{Sc}$ and $\text{Zn}$ are NOT transition metals ($\text{Sc}^{3+}$ is $3d^0$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ is $3d^{10}$).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Never state "partially filled $d$-shell in the atom". The IAL mark scheme strictly requires "in an ION" or "forms one or more stable ions with partially filled $d$-orbitals". Also watch out for Chromium ($\text{Cr}: 4s^1 3d^5$) and Copper ($\text{Cu}: 4s^1 3d^{10}$) atomic configuration exceptions!

Spec 17.4 - 17.6 Complex Ions & Ligands

2. Complex Formation, Ligands & Coordination Number

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Define a **Ligand**: An atom, ion, or molecule with a lone pair that forms a dative (coordinate) covalent bond to a central metal ion.
  • Define a **Complex Ion**: A central metal ion surrounded by ligands bound by coordinate bonds.
  • Define **Coordination Number**: The total number of dative bonds formed with the central metal ion (NOT just total ligands!).
  • Classify Ligand Denticity: Monodentate ($\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{NH}_3, \text{Cl}^-$), Bidentate ($\text{en}, \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$), and Hexadentate ($\text{EDTA}^{4-}$).
  • Identify 3D Complex Shapes: Octahedral (CN=6), Tetrahedral (CN=4), Square Planar ($\text{Pt}^{2+}$, CN=4), Linear ($\text{Ag}^+$, CN=2).
💡 IAL Examiner Watch-Outs:

Coordination number is the number of DATIVE BONDS, not number of molecules! E.g. $[\text{Cr}(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_3]^{3-}$ has 3 ligands but its coordination number is 6 because ethanedioate is bidentate. Also, anionic complex names end in "-ate" (e.g., tetrachloroferrate(III)).

Spec 17.7 - 17.9 d-Orbital Splitting & Color

3. Origin of Colour in Transition Metal Complexes (4-Mark Core Answer)

🎯 5 Key Learning Goals:
  • Understand that ligands approaching a central metal ion split the $3d$ orbitals into two distinct energy levels.
  • Explain $d$-$d$ transitions: Electrons absorb specific frequencies/wavelengths of visible light ($\Delta E = h\nu$) and are promoted from lower to higher $d$-orbitals.
  • Explain complementary colours: The unabsorbed frequencies of visible light are **transmitted/reflected**, giving the solution its observed colour.
  • Explain colour variations ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$ vs $\text{Fe}^{3+}$): Different metal oxidation states/ligands alter $\Delta E$, absorbing different frequencies.
  • Explain colourless ions ($\text{Sc}^{3+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}$): $\text{Sc}^{3+}$ has no $d$-electrons to promote ($3d^0$), and $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ has completely filled $d$-orbitals ($3d^{10}$) with no empty level for promotion.
📝 Official 4-Mark Exam Marking Scheme Checklist for "Why is a complex ion coloured?":
1. Ligands split $3d$ orbitals into two energy levels.
2. Electrons absorb specific frequencies of visible light.
3. Electron promoted from lower to higher $d$-orbital ($d$-$d$ transition).
4. Remaining/complementary light is transmitted.

⚖️ IAL Exam Classification Analysis

4s Electron Loss Rule:

When transition metals form positive ions, electrons are ALWAYS lost from $4s$ FIRST before $3d$ electrons! E.g. $\text{Fe} (3d^6 4s^2) \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{2+} (3d^6 4s^0) \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{3+} (3d^5 4s^0)$.

Spec 17.1 - 17.2

Official IAL Syllabus Proof: Why Sc & Zn Are NOT Transition Metals

Target Mark Scheme Key Phrase: "Incompletely Filled d-Orbital in an ION"
1. Scandium (Sc, Z = 21) ❌ NOT A TRANSITION METAL
• Atom Ground State: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^1 4s^2 \Rightarrow [\text{Ar}] 3d^1 4s^2$
• Only Stable Ion: $\text{Sc}^{3+} : [\text{Ar}] 3d^0 4s^0$
• $3d$ Subshell Status: EMPTY ($3d^0$)
IAL Mark Scheme Answer:
"Scandium forms only one stable ion, $\text{Sc}^{3+}$. Its electronic configuration is $[\text{Ar}] 3d^0$. Because its $d$-subshell is EMPTY ($3d^0$), it does NOT have a partially filled $d$-orbital in any stable ion."
2. Zinc (Zn, Z = 30) ❌ NOT A TRANSITION METAL
• Atom Ground State: $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^{10} 4s^2 \Rightarrow [\text{Ar}] 3d^{10} 4s^2$
• Only Stable Ion: $\text{Zn}^{2+} : [\text{Ar}] 3d^{10} 4s^0$
• $3d$ Subshell Status: COMPLETELY FILLED ($3d^{10}$)
IAL Mark Scheme Answer:
"Zinc forms only one stable ion, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$. Its electronic configuration is $[\text{Ar}] 3d^{10}$. Because its $d$-subshell is FULL ($3d^{10}$), it does NOT have an incompletely filled $d$-orbital. $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ compounds are COLOURLESS!"
💡 Why is Copper (Cu) a Transition Metal even though Cu⁺ is 3d¹⁰?

Copper forms two stable ions: $\text{Cu}^+ (3d^{10})$ and $\text{Cu}^{2+} (3d^9)$. Because $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ has a partially filled $3d^9$ subshell, Copper satisfies the definition ("forms ONE OR MORE stable ions...").

How to Deduce Central Metal Oxidation States in Any Complex

Step-by-step algebraic method for neutral, cationic, anionic, and multi-ligand complexes.

Formula: $x + \sum (\text{Charge}_{\text{ligand}} \times n) = \text{Charge}_{\text{overall}}$
1. Neutral Ligands (Charge = 0)

Do NOT alter the oxidation state of the metal ion!

  • Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ aqua): $\text{Charge} = 0$
  • Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$ ammine): $\text{Charge} = 0$
  • Carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$ carbonyl): $\text{Charge} = 0$
  • 1,2-diaminoethane ($\text{en}$): $\text{Charge} = 0$
2. Single Negative (-1) Ligands

Each ligand contributes $-1$ to the overall charge math.

  • Chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$ chloro): $\text{Charge} = -1$
  • Hydroxide ($\text{OH}^-$ hydroxo): $\text{Charge} = -1$
  • Cyanide ($\text{CN}^-$ cyano): $\text{Charge} = -1$
  • Fluoride ($\text{F}^-$) / Bromide ($\text{Br}^-$): $-1$
3. Multi-Negative (-2 / -4) Ligands

Anionic multidentate species with larger negative charges.

  • Ethanedioate ($\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$): $\text{Charge} = -2$
  • Sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$): $\text{Charge} = -2$
  • Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$): $\text{Charge} = -2$
  • $\text{EDTA}^{4-}$ (Hexadentate): $\text{Charge} = -4$
Case A: Complex Ion with Mixed Ligands

Example: $[\text{Co}(\text{NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^+$
Let metal oxidation state $= x$.
Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) is neutral ($0$), Chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$) is $-1$. Overall complex charge $= +1$.

$x + 4(0) + 2(-1) = +1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x - 2 = +1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = +3$
Case B: Neutral Ionic Salt Containing Complex Anion

Example: $\text{K}_2[\text{PdCl}_6]$
Overall salt is neutral ($0$). Potassium is Group 1 ($\text{K}^+ = +1$).
Two $\text{K}^+$ ions carry $+2$, so complex anion $[\text{PdCl}_6]^{2-}$ must have charge $-2$.

$2(+1) + [x + 6(-1)] = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad +2 + x - 6 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = +4$
