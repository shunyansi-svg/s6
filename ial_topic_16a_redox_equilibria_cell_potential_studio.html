Cathode (Positive Electrode)

🐱 RED-CAT Rule

REDUCTION always occurs at the CATHODE.

  • Accepts electrons ($e^-$) from external wire.
  • Contains the species with the more positive $E^\ominus$.
  • Example: $\text{Cu}^{2+}(\text{aq}) + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}(\text{s})$
Anode (Negative Electrode)

🐂 OX-AN Rule

OXIDATION always occurs at the ANODE.

  • Releases electrons ($e^-$) into external wire.
  • Contains the species with the more negative $E^\ominus$.
  • Example: $\text{Zn}(\text{s}) \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}(\text{aq}) + 2e^-$
Standard Conditions ($E^\ominus$)

🌡️ Mandatory Conditions

Measured against Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE = 0.00 V).

  • Temperature: $298\text{ K}$ ($25^\circ\text{C}$)
  • Gas Pressure: $100\text{ kPa}$ ($1\text{ atm}$)
  • Ion Concentration: $1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$

🧪 Salt Bridge: Preparation & 3 Mandatory Functions (Must-Know Exam Criteria)

A salt bridge is made by soaking filter paper in a saturated potassium nitrate ($\text{KNO}_3$) solution. Both $\text{K}^+$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$ salts are soluble in water, ensuring no precipitate interferes with the mobile ions.

Function 1: Prevents Direct Mixing Prevents direct mixing and chemical reaction between the two electrolyte solutions in the half-cells.
Function 2: Completes the Circuit Completes the electrical circuit by providing **mobile ions** ($\text{K}^+$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$) that move between half-cells (electrons ONLY flow through the wire!).
Function 3: Neutralizes Charge Buildup Supplies $\text{K}^+$ ions to the cathode (to replace reduced cations) and $\text{NO}_3^-$ to the anode (to balance accumulated oxidation cations).

⚠️ Key IAL Exam Pitfalls & Examiner Marking Tricks

1. Why NOT Immerse Metal Directly in Opposite Electrolyte? If $\text{Zn}$ metal is dipped directly into $\text{Cu}^{2+}(\text{aq})$, a **direct displacement reaction** occurs on the metal surface. Heat energy is released directly, bypassing the wire and destroying the measurable voltage output!
2. Platinum ($\text{Pt}$) Inert Electrode Requirement For non-metal half-cells ($\text{Cl}_2/\text{Cl}^-$) or ion-ion half-cells ($\text{Fe}^{3+}/\text{Fe}^{2+}$), a **platinum electrode** is used because it conducts electricity, provides a reaction surface, but is **chemically inert**.

⚡ Salt Bridge Charge-Balancing Mechanics (Why Reaction Continues)
🐂 Anode Beaker (Oxidation):

Oxidation forms positive cations ($\text{Zn}^{2+}$). Excess positive charge buildup would repel incoming current and halt reaction! **$\text{NO}_3^-$ anions** migrate out of salt bridge into this beaker to neutralize positive charge.

🐱 Cathode Beaker (Reduction):

Reduction consumes aqueous cations ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$), leaving excess negative counter-ions ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$). **$\text{K}^+$ cations** migrate out of salt bridge into this beaker to restore charge balance.

Wire: Electrons (e⁻) flow Anode → Cathode
Salt Bridge: K⁺ Cations enter Cathode Beaker
Salt Bridge: NO₃⁻ Anions enter Anode Beaker

Why Platinised Platinum Black?

Platinum is chosen because it is metallic and conducts electrons, but is **chemically inert** (will not react with $\text{H}^+$ or $\text{H}_2$). Coating it with porous "platinum black" drastically increases the surface area to allow rapid equilibrium between $\text{H}_2(\text{g})$ gas and $\text{H}^+(\text{aq})$ ions.
