Topic 19 Organic Nitrogen Compounds: Specification Goals

Detailed breakdown of Edexcel IAL Specification points 19.1 through 19.6 with explicit learning outcomes and mark scheme criteria.

19.1 Nomenclature & Structures

Naming & Functional Groups

  • Name $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines using the -amine suffix or amino- prefix.
  • Use $N$-alkylation nomenclature (e.g. $N$-methylethanamide, $N,N$-dimethylpropan-1-amine).
  • Draw skeletal, displayed, and structural formulae for aliphatic and aromatic amines.
  • Classify quaternary ammonium salts ($[\text{R}_4\text{N}]^+\text{X}^-$).
19.2 Core Amine Reactions

Aliphatic vs Aromatic Reactivity

  • Reaction with water to form alkaline solutions ($\text{pH} \approx 11-12$ vs $8-9$).
  • Neutralization with mineral acids to form ionic ammonium salts.
  • Nucleophilic substitution with halogenoalkanes forming $2^\circ/3^\circ$ amines.
  • Nucleophilic addition-elimination with acyl chlorides to form amides.
  • Complexation with $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions (deprotonation ppt $\rightarrow$ ligand exchange).
19.3 Solubility & Basicity

Hydrogen Bonding & Lone Pair

  • Explain water solubility of lower amines via hydrogen bonding ($\text{N-H}\cdots\text{O}$).
  • Define basicity in terms of Nitrogen lone pair availability to accept a proton ($\text{H}^+$).
  • Master basicity order: $2^\circ \text{ Amine} > 1^\circ \text{ Amine} > 3^\circ \text{ Amine} > \text{NH}_3 > \text{Phenylamine}$.
  • Contrast $+I$ inductive effect in alkylamines vs $\pi$-delocalisation in phenylamine.
19.4 Aliphatic Preparation

Haloalkanes vs Nitrile Reduction

  • Preparation from haloalkane $+$ excess ethanolic ammonia in a sealed tube under pressure.
  • Explain why excess $\text{NH}_3$ minimizes multi-substitution side products.
  • Nitrile reduction via $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in dry ether or $\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ catalytic hydrogenation.
  • Highlight $+1$ carbon chain extension route ($\text{RX} \xrightarrow{\text{KCN}} \text{R-CN} \rightarrow \text{R-CH}_2\text{NH}_2$).
19.5 Aromatic Preparation

Reduction of Nitrobenzene

  • Conversion of nitrobenzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2$) to phenylamine ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$).
  • Reagents: Tin ($\text{Sn}$) and concentrated hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$) heated under reflux.
  • Neutralization of phenylammonium salt using aqueous sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$).
  • Purification of oily phenylamine via steam distillation and solvent extraction.
19.6 Diazotisation & Dyes

Diazonium Coupling Chemistry

  • Formation of benzenediazonium chloride from phenylamine $+$ nitrous acid ($\text{NaNO}_2+\text{HCl}$).
  • Strict temperature control: $<10^\circ\text{C}$ (strictly $0-10^\circ\text{C}$) to prevent phenol formation.
  • Electrophilic coupling with phenol in alkaline $\text{NaOH}$ forming phenoxide ion.
  • Bright orange 4-phenylazophenol precipitate dye featuring $-\text{N=N}-$ azo link.

Preparation Routes for Aliphatic & Aromatic Amines

Compare haloalkane substitution, nitrile reduction (+1 carbon chain extension), and nitrobenzene reduction.

Method 1: Low Yield

Haloalkane + Ammonia

Nucleophilic substitution using excess ethanolic $\text{NH}_3$ in a sealed tube under pressure.

$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + 2\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Br}$

⚠️ Multi-Substitution Trap: Primary amine product remains nucleophilic and attacks remaining haloalkane to form $2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines and $4^\circ$ quaternary salts ($[\text{R}_4\text{N}]^+\text{X}^-$). Use excess $\text{NH}_3$ to maximize $1^\circ$ amine yield!
Method 2: Pure 1° Amine

Nitrile Reduction (+1 C)

2-Step synthetic route converting haloalkanes to primary amines with a $+1$ carbon chain extension.

Step 1: $\text{R-Cl} + \text{KCN (ethanolic)} \rightarrow \text{R-CN} + \text{KCl}$

Step 2: $\text{R-CN} + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 / \text{dry ether}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{NH}_2$

💡 Advantage: Produces 100% pure primary amine with NO side products! Alternative reduction: $\text{H}_2(\text{g}) / \text{Ni catalyst}$ (catalytic hydrogenation).
Method 3: Aromatic Route

Reduction of Nitrobenzene

Preparation of phenylamine from nitrobenzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2$).

Step 1: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{Sn / conc. HCl, reflux}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_3]^+\text{Cl}^-$

Step 2: $[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_3]^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

📌 Purification Note: Phenylamine is an oily liquid separated by steam distillation, followed by solvent extraction using ether and drying with anhydrous $\text{MgSO}_4$.

13 Core Reactions of Aliphatic & Aromatic Amines

Complete IAL revision matrix listing equations, visual observations, reacting conditions, examiner watch-outs, and common traps.

Reaction # Reactants & Conditions Balanced Chemical Equation Visual Observations IAL Examiner Watch-Out & Traps
1. Amine + Water Aliphatic Amine $+\text{H}_2\text{O}$
Room Temp		 $\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons [\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_3]^+ + \text{OH}^-$ Alkaline solution ($\text{pH} \approx 11-12$). Fishy smell. Use reversible equilibrium arrows ($\rightleftharpoons$). Phenylamine yields much weaker alkaline solution ($\text{pH} \approx 8$).
2. Amine + HCl Amine $+\text{HCl}(\text{aq})$
Room Temp		 $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{HCl} \rightarrow [\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_3]^+\text{Cl}^-$ Insoluble oily phenylamine dissolves to form clear solution. Adding $\text{NaOH}$ neutralizes $\text{H}^+$ and regenerates insoluble oily phenylamine.
3. Amine + H₂SO₄ Amine $+\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{aq})$
Room Temp		 $2\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow [(\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_3)_2]^{2+}\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ Clear ionic salt solution formed. Mole Ratio Trap: Sulfuric acid is diprotic (2:1 amine:acid ratio). Always write $[(\text{RNH}_3)_2]^{2+}\text{SO}_4^{2-}$.
4. Excess NH₃ + RX Excess $\text{NH}_3 + \text{RX}$
Ethanol, Sealed Tube		 $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Cl} + 2\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ Primary amine formed in high yield. Ethanolic solvent prevents water from competing as a nucleophile to form alcohols.
5. Excess RX + NH₃ Excess Haloalkane $+\text{NH}_3$
Ethanol, Heat		 $4\text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{NH}_3 \rightarrow [(\text{CH}_3)_4\text{N}]^+\text{Cl}^- + 3\text{HCl}$ Yields mixture of $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines and quaternary salt. Product amines remain nucleophilic. Quaternary ammonium salts are used industrially as cationic surfactants.
6. Acyl Chloride + NH₃ $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + \text{NH}_3$
Room Temp (Violent)		 $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + 2\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ Vigorous reaction, steamy white fumes of $\text{HCl}$ / white solid salt. Forms a primary amide ($\text{R-CONH}_2$). Nucleophilic addition-elimination mechanism.
7. Acyl Chloride + 1° Amine $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + \text{RNH}_2$
Room Temp		 $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CONHCH}_3 + \text{HCl}$ White crystalline solid product ($N$-substituted amide). Forms a secondary amide ($N$-methylacetamide). Used in Paracetamol preparation.
8. Acyl Chloride + 2° Amine $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + \text{R}_2\text{NH}$
Room Temp		 $\text{CH}_3\text{COCl} + (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CON}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{HCl}$ Forms a tertiary amide. Tertiary Amine Trap: Tertiary amines $(\text{R}_3\text{N})$ CANNOT react with acyl chlorides because Nitrogen lacks an $\text{H}$ atom to eliminate!
9. Amine + Cu²⁺ (Dropwise) Dropwise Amine $+ [\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$
Room Temp		 $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+} + 2\text{RNH}_2 \rightarrow \text{Cu}(\text{OH})_2(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4(\text{s}) + 2[\text{RNH}_3]^+$ Pale blue precipitate forms. Dropwise amine acts as a base (deprotonation extracting $\text{H}^+$ from water ligands).
10. Amine + Cu²⁺ (Excess) Excess Amine $+ [\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$
Room Temp		 $\text{Cu}(\text{OH})_2(\text{H}_2\text{O})_4 + 4\text{RNH}_2 \rightarrow [\text{Cu}(\text{RNH}_2)_4(\text{H}_2\text{O})_2]^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Blue precipitate dissolves to form a deep royal blue solution. Excess amine acts as a ligand (ligand exchange). Only 4 amine molecules coordinate to $\text{Cu}^{2+}$.
11. Diazotisation $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$
Strictly 0°C – 10°C		 $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{HNO}_2 + \text{HCl} \xrightarrow{<10^\circ\text{C}} [\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2]^+\text{Cl}^- + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ Benzenediazonium chloride solution formed. Temperature Trap: Above $10^\circ\text{C}$, diazonium ion decomposes into phenol releasing $\text{N}_2(\text{g})$ bubbles! $\text{HNO}_2$ prepared in situ.
12. Azo Coupling (Phenol) Diazonium Ion $+$ Phenol in $\text{NaOH}$
Cold Alkaline Solution		 $[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2]^+ + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^- \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-N=N-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{OH} + \text{H}^+$ Bright orange/red precipitate dye (4-phenylazophenol). Phenol must be dissolved in alkaline $\text{NaOH}$ to form phenoxide ion ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{O}^-$), activating the ring for electrophilic attack.
13. Azo Coupling (Amine) Diazonium Ion $+ \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)_2$
Cold Solution		 $[\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2]^+ + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{-N=N-C}_6\text{H}_4\text{N}(\text{CH}_3)_2 + \text{H}^+$ Bright yellow dye formed (Methyl Yellow / Methyl Orange derivative). Demonstrates coupling with tertiary aromatic amines to prepare pH indicators. Extended delocalised $\pi$-system absorbs visible light.
