✨ Molecule Symmetry & Peak Reduction

Key Concept

Molecules that possess internal symmetry display fewer $^{13}\text{C}$ NMR peaks ($\delta$) than the total number of carbon atoms in the molecule.

Classic Examples from Teacher Notes:

  • 1,2-cyclohexanediol: Contains 6 carbons but displays only 3 peaks due to its axial line of symmetry.
  • 1,4-cyclohexanediol: Highly symmetrical cyclic diol containing 6 carbons but showing only 2 peaks!
  • 2,2-dimethylpropane: All 4 methyl carbons are in identical environments attached to a central quaternary carbon $\rightarrow$ 1 peak total.

🛑 Benzene Ring Carbon Environments

Exam Frequent

In aromatic compounds, substituent positions dictate ring symmetry:

Benzene (C6H6): 1 peak
Toluene (C6H5CH3): 5 peaks
Phenol / Aniline: 4 peaks
Salicylic acid: 7 peaks
Hydroquinone (1,4-benzenediol): 2 peaks

🍌 Case Study: Isoamyl vs Amyl Acetate

How to distinguish structural isomers using peak counting:

• Isoamyl acetate (Banana oil): Has a branched terminal isopropyl group $(-\text{CH(CH}_3)_2)$. The two methyl carbons are symmetrical and equivalent $\rightarrow$ 6 peaks.

• Amyl acetate (Apple scent): Unbranched linear chain $(-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3)$. Every carbon atom is unique $\rightarrow$ 7 peaks.
