IUPAC Conventional Cell Diagram Rules & Thermodynamic Relationships

Master cell notation formatting, $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$ calculation rules, and entropy proportionalities for Edexcel IAL exams.

LHS = Oxidation (Anode)

🐂 Left Hand Side (Oxidation)

Species being oxidized (releasing $e^-$).

  • Reducing agent written on far left.
  • Phase boundary: Single solid vertical line `|`.
  • Example: $\text{Zn(s)} \mid [\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)}]$
RHS = Reduction (Cathode)

🐱 Right Hand Side (Reduction)

Species being reduced (accepting $e^-$).

  • Oxidizing agent written on right.
  • Salt bridge: Double solid vertical line `||`.
  • Example: $[\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)}] \mid \text{Cu(s)}$
Inert Platinum Electrode

⚡ Platinum ($\text{Pt(s)}$) Rule

Required when no conducting metal electrode is present.

  • Ion-ion cells in same phase: $\text{Pt(s)} \mid [\text{Fe}^{2+}\text{(aq)}] , [\text{Fe}^{3+}\text{(aq)}]$
  • Dichromate/Permanganate: $[\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 14\text{H}^+\text{(aq)}] , [2\text{Cr}^{3+}\text{(aq)} + 7\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}] \mid \text{Pt(s)}$
  • Gas half-cells: $\text{Pt(s)} \mid \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \mid [2\text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)}]$
  • Mandatory Rule: Species in same environment must use `[species(aq)]` brackets with `,`.

⭐ Mandatory Edexcel Mark Scheme Rules for $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$

1. $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$ Formula $$E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{RHS}} - E^\ominus_{\text{LHS}} = E^\ominus_{\text{reduction}} - E^\ominus_{\text{oxidation}}$$ Alternatively: Swap the equation for the more negative species and ADD potentials ($E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} > 0$ for feasible reactions).
2. CRITICAL EXAM PITFALL: DO NOT MULTIPLY $E^\ominus$! When balancing overall stoichiometry (e.g., multiplying a half-equation by 2 or 5), **NEVER multiply the $E^\ominus$ value!** $E^\ominus$ is an intensive property (potential difference per unit charge).

📐 Thermodynamic Proportionalities (Specification 16.11)

Standard cell potential $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$ is directly proportional to both total entropy change ($\Delta S_{\text{total}}$) and the natural logarithm of the equilibrium constant ($\ln K$).

1. Total Entropy Change ($\Delta S_{\text{total}}$)
$$\Delta S_{\text{total}} = n F E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$$
$$\Delta S_{\text{total}} \propto E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$$

Where $n$ = moles of electrons transferred, $F$ = Faraday constant ($96,500\text{ C mol}^{-1}$). Since $n$ and $F$ are positive, a positive $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$ guarantees a positive $\Delta S_{\text{total}}$.

2. Equilibrium Constant ($\ln K$)
$$\ln K = \frac{n F E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}}{R T}$$
$$\ln K \propto E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$$

A larger positive $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$ yields a significantly larger $K$ value, indicating that the reaction goes virtually to completion at equilibrium.

⚠️ Limitations of $E^\ominus$ Feasibility Predictions (Spec 16.12)

1. Kinetic Stability (High Activation Energy $E_a$): A reaction may be thermodynamically feasible ($E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} > 0$), but occurs at an unmeasurably slow rate due to a high activation energy barrier $E_a$.
2. Departure from Standard Conditions: If ion concentrations deviate from $1.00\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ or temperature differs from $298\text{ K}$, Le Chatelier shifts alter $E$ away from $E^\ominus$, potentially changing feasibility.

⚠️ Why does Zinc stop at $\text{V}^{2+}$ and NOT reduce to $\text{V(s)}$ metal?

For $\text{V}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightleftharpoons \text{V(s)}$, $E^\ominus = -1.18\text{ V}$. Paired with Zinc oxidation ($\text{Zn} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-, E^\ominus = +0.76\text{ V}$): $$E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = -1.18 - (-0.76) = -0.42\text{ V}$$ Because $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} < 0$, reducing $\text{V}^{2+}$ to $\text{V(s)}$ is **thermodynamically non-feasible** under standard conditions!

♻️ Disproportionation Analysis: Hydrogen Peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$)

Disproportionation occurs when a single species is simultaneously **oxidized and reduced** in the same reaction.

$$2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2(\text{aq}) \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g})$$
Oxidation: $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}^+ + 2e^-$
Oxidation number of O: $-1 \rightarrow 0$
Reduction: $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
Oxidation number of O: $-1 \rightarrow -2$
$$E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = +1.77\text{ V} - (+0.68\text{ V}) = +1.09\text{ V} > 0 \text{ (Feasible!)}$$
