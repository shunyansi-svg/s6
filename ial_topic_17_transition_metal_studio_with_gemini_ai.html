a. Hydrolysis Acidity Comparison ($\text{M}^{3+}$ vs $\text{M}^{2+}$ Aqua-Ions)

Key Concept: $\text{pH: } \text{FeCl}_3 < \text{FeCl}_2$
$\text{M}^{3+}$ Aqua-Ions (e.g. $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$, $[\text{Cr}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}$):
$[\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{3+}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightleftharpoons [\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{OH})]^{2+}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{aq})$

Noticeably more acidic ($\text{pH} \approx 2-3$). High charge density / charge-to-size ratio gives strong polarizing power. This pulls electron density from $\text{O-H}$ bonds in water ligands, releasing $\text{H}^+$ protons into solution.

$\text{M}^{2+}$ Aqua-Ions (e.g. $[\text{Fe}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$, $[\text{Cu}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$):
$[\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightleftharpoons [\text{M}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_5(\text{OH})]^+(\text{aq}) + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+(\text{aq})$

Only weakly acidic ($\text{pH} \approx 5-6$). Lower charge density results in weaker polarizing power, so water ligands retain their $\text{H}^+$ protons much more strongly.

a. Heterogeneous Catalysis Mechanics (Surface Active Sites)

A solid catalyst operates via 3 sequential stages on its surface:

Stage 1: Adsorption

Reactants bond to active sites on the catalyst surface. This increases local proximity of molecules and weakens covalent bonds within reactants.

Stage 2: Reaction

Adsorbed molecules react on the surface with significantly lower activation energy ($E_a$).

Stage 3: Desorption

Product molecules detach/desorb from active sites, freeing the catalyst surface to adsorb new reactant molecules.

Industrial Process

Manufacture of Conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (Contact Process)

All 4 Chemical Equations:

Step 1 (Combustion): $\text{S}(\text{s}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow \text{SO}_2(\text{g})$

Step 2 (Contact Reaction): $2\text{SO}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \xrightleftharpoons[\text{V}_2\text{O}_5, 450^\circ\text{C}]{} 2\text{SO}_3(\text{g})$

Step 3 (Oleum Formation): $\text{SO}_3(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{l}) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7(\text{l})$

Step 4 (Hydration to Acid): $\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7(\text{l}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(\text{l})$

$\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ Catalyst Mechanism:

1. $\text{SO}_2 + \text{V}_2\text{O}_5 \rightarrow \text{SO}_3 + \text{V}_2\text{O}_4$ ($\text{V}^{5+} \rightarrow \text{V}^{4+}$)
2. $2\text{V}_2\text{O}_4 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ ($\text{V}^{4+} \rightarrow \text{V}^{5+}$)

Car Exhaust Catalysis

Catalytic Converters ($\text{Pt} / \text{Pd} / \text{Rh}$)

Coated on a ceramic honeycomb support structure to dramatically increase surface area while minimizing the expensive catalyst mass.

Exhaust Conversion Equations:

$\text{C}_x\text{H}_y + \left(x + \frac{y}{4}\right)\text{O}_2 \rightarrow x\text{CO}_2 + \frac{y}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Autocatalysis Masterclass

Ethanedioate Titration ($\text{MnO}_4^- + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ in Acid)

Autocatalysis: The product of the reaction ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$) acts as the catalyst for the reaction!

Overall Reaction & Why Initial Rate is Slow:
$2\text{MnO}_4^- + 5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} + 16\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 2\text{Mn}^{2+} + 10\text{CO}_2 + 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Why slow initially? Both $\text{MnO}_4^-$ and $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ are negatively charged ions. They strongly repel each other, resulting in a very high activation energy barrier without catalyst!

Intermediate Reactions ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$ Catalytic Cycle):

Step 1: $4\text{Mn}^{2+} + \text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 5\text{Mn}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Step 2: $2\text{Mn}^{3+} + \text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{Mn}^{2+}$

$\text{Mn}^{2+}$ reduces $\text{MnO}_4^-$ to $\text{Mn}^{3+}$, which then oxidizes ethanedioate $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$ back to $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ and $\text{CO}_2$.

Sigmoidal Rate Curve Breakdown:

1. Slow Start: No catalyst ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$) present initially; high anion repulsion barrier.

2. Rapid Acceleration: As $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ forms, it catalyzes the reaction, rapidly speeding up reactant consumption.

3. Slowing Down & Stopping: Reactants ($\text{MnO}_4^-$ and $\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$) are consumed, so rate drops to zero.
